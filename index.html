    How ScamShield thinks

    An explainable rule engine, not a black box. Every point of risk comes with a reason you can read.

    1

    Normalise

    Unicode NFC, lower-case and diacritic folding, so “khóa tài khoản” and “khoa tai khoan” match the same rule. Character positions are kept 1:1 for highlighting.

    2

    Detect tactics

    – bilingual patterns across – manipulation tactics: urgency, threats, secret requests, untraceable payments, impersonation…

    3

    Inspect links

    Look-alike detection (Levenshtein distance + leet-speak decoding), brand-in-subdomain, punycode, raw IPs, shorteners and abused TLDs.

    4

    Score & explain

    Diminishing returns per rule, combination boosts (e.g. link + password request), squashed to 0–100, then mapped to a scam type with tailored advice.

    Why it matters
    • Fraud is now the most common crime that people report in many countries, and most of it begins with a single message.
    • Older adults, students and first-time internet users are hit hardest, and they're often too embarrassed to ask anyone for help.
    • The same tactics show up in every language. ScamShield teaches people to recognise the tactic, not just one particular message.
    Privacy by design
    • No server, no account, no tracking: the engine is plain JavaScript running in your browser.
    • Works offline once loaded, so it's safe to paste bank messages and personal details.
    • Open source and unit-tested, so anyone can audit or extend the rules.
